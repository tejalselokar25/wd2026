Albert Einstein

Albert Einstein was a theoretical physicist who developed the theory of relativity, one of the two pillars of modern physics (alongside quantum mechanics). His work has had a profound effect on the way we understand space, time, and energy.

Einstein is best known for his equation E = mc², which expresses the idea that energy and mass are interchangeable. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921 for his explanation of the photoelectric effect, which was pivotal in the development of quantum theory.
